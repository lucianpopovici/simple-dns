# P022 — The Maginot Line: Attacking the Boundary of DNS Caching Protection (MaginotDNS)

- Type/credibility: peer-reviewed
- Subtopic: cache-poisoning
- Source: USENIX Security, 2023 · https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/li-xiang
- Status: have-meta · summary: verified

**Research question:** Is the boundary between DNS forwarding and recursive caching (CDNS) a cache-poisoning vector?
**Method:** Analysis of bailiwick-checking in popular DNS software plus Internet measurement of CDNS prevalence and vulnerability.
**Main findings:** Forwarder-mode bailiwick checks are weakly enforced; because forwarder and recursive modes share one cache, an attack on the forwarder breaches the recursive cache and can hijack entire zones (incl. TLDs). 41.8% of probed open resolvers are CDNS; >=35.5% of those are vulnerable; affects BIND and Microsoft DNS.
**Key evidence:** Proof-of-concept + Internet-wide scan.
**Limitations:** Requires CDNS configuration; patched in affected software after disclosure.
**Relevance to this project:** Bailiwick-check flaw in forwarder+recursive (CDNS) — can poison TLDs; affects BIND, MS DNS
