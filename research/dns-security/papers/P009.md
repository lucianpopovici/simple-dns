# P009 — DNS Cache Poisoning Attack Reloaded: Revolutions with Side Channels

- Type/credibility: peer-reviewed
- Subtopic: cache-poisoning
- Source: ACM CCS, 2020 · https://doi.org/10.1145/3372297.3417280
- Status: have-meta · summary: verified

**Research question:** Can classic DNS cache poisoning be revived against modern resolvers with randomized source ports and TXIDs?
**Method:** Discovery of network side channels (shared ICMP error/rate-limit state) to infer the ephemeral source port off-path; full attack construction; Internet measurement of vulnerable resolvers/OSes.
**Main findings:** The side channel reveals the source port, making TXID brute-force feasible; affects Linux/Windows/macOS/FreeBSD; a large fraction of open resolvers and forwarders are vulnerable. (CCS'20 Distinguished Paper.)
**Key evidence:** Working proof-of-concept plus measurement of affected populations.
**Limitations:** Depends on specific ICMP rate-limit behaviour; mitigations (randomized limits, etc.) exist.
**Relevance to this project:** SAD DNS — ICMP side channel revives cache poisoning (Distinguished Paper)
